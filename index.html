The 3 components of rally power 
Rally power = Stats × Buffs × Skills

1. Stats. Your battle report % from gear, research, charms and heroes. They work in two pairs: (Attack × Lethality) and (Defense × Health). Inside each pair a point helps most on the lower stat, so raise the lower stat of each pair first. Joiners can't fix this.

Example: Attack 5 × Lethality 3 = 15
  +1 Attack:     6 × 3 = 18
  +1 Lethality:  5 × 4 = 20   increasing the lower stat gains more

2. Buffs (other multipliers). Widgets, pets, city buffs and titles (King, Marshal and so on). Same-stat buffs add up, then multiply your stats. Enemy-down buffs shrink their stat. In a rally, only the leader's widgets count.

3. Skills. The lead heroes' skills plus each joiner's first skill. Same-stat bonuses add up in one bucket, and the buckets multiply. This page only covers this part.

Kills scale with:  your stats ÷ enemy stats  ×  skill boost
                    └ fixed by you ────────┘     └ joiners only change this

Your stats are already fixed, so this page sets them aside.

• A joiner's worth is relative: skill boost with them ÷ skill boost without them
    Example: 2 lethality joiners at 25% already give +50%.
    A 3rd one: 1.75 ÷ 1.50, so its real gain is +16.7%, not the +25% on its card.

• PvP counts both sides:
  Damage you deal = (Attack × Lethality) ÷ (their Defense × Health) × your offense buckets
  Damage you take = (their Attack × Lethality) ÷ (Defense × Health) ÷ your defense buckets

So two players with the same three lead heroes want the same joiners, whatever their stats. Each joiner brings its own multiplier, and crowding decides its real gain.

What each copy adds, copies 1 to 7

Not every copy of a skill counts the same. On paper (its nominal value), every 25% copy adds +25%. Its real gain is relative: it is measured against the group total before it, so each new copy is a smaller step up from a bigger total.

Up to 3 copies can come from the lead team, then up to 4 from joiners, so 7 is the most a group can hold. Copy 1 (highlighted) is a skill going into an empty group. A fresh 20% (+20%) ties a 25% that would be copy 2, and beats any 25% that would be copy 3 or later. Within one group, every 25% copy still adds the same 25 points: with 100 damage, copy 1 takes it to 125 and copy 2 to 150. The percentage shrinks because each copy is measured against a bigger total. The same copy in a different group would multiply instead (125 × 1.25 = 156.25), so crowding costs about 6 damage per 100 here, and more with each copy.

Your lead team: pick a group

Bonuses to the same stat (attack, lethality, defense, health, and so on) add together inside one bucket. The buckets then multiply together for the final result. So each extra copy in a bucket is a smaller step up, and a joiner only adds its full card value when its bucket is empty.

Row 0 is the lead team (rally or garrison): every skill its three heroes have in this group. Rows 1–4 are joiners added one at a time; only 4 joiner skills count. Real gain is how much that row raises the result. For debuffs, "enemy left" shows the same thing from their side. A −25% card (Ava) leaves them 80%, the same as +25% for you, not 75%. A −20% card (Fahd, Eric) leaves 83%, the same as +20%.

Does a weaker player gain more from debuffs?

No. Kills come from your stats divided by theirs, so a cut to their side and a boost to yours move the result by the same amount.

You 1,500% vs them 4,000%

Start             1,500 ÷ 4,000 = 0.375
You ×1.25         1,875 ÷ 4,000 = 0.469
Them ÷1.25        1,500 ÷ 3,200 = 0.469   same result

So the size of anyone's stats never decides between a buff and a debuff. Two things do: the size of the skill (Fahd and Eric are 20%, so they divide by 1.2), and how full its group already is.

Takeaway: always pick the joiner with the largest relative increase, whether it buffs you or debuffs them.

Petra: her skill makes one 50% check per troop type; if any check succeeds, the +50% applies. With 3 types she triggers 87.5% of the time (about 43.75% on average), with 2 types 75% (about 37.5%). It is its own skill group ("enemy damage taken up"), separate from lethality, so she never crowds Chenko or Yeonwoo. The group does nothing on the first turn of a battle; Bear lasts 10 rounds, so her Bear value is 9/10 of the above (39.4% with 3 types). Source: Sloth and Frakinator's engineered test battles (2026). Only one Petra counts, and she is dropped if the lead team already has her. The troop types picker at the top only changes this Petra number.

Consistent results: joiners are picked for consistent, repeatable results, so chance-based skills are normally left out. Petra is the exception: 87.5% is close to always on, where other chance skills can be as low as 40%. Chance and turn-based lead skills (for example "40% chance: damage dealt +50%") are listed but not counted, because nobody has published their skill group. The one exception is Rosa's Chaos Gambit, which Sloth and Frakinator tested as a stacking lethality skill: it counts at its 20% average.

Which joiners count: the first four whose first skill is level 5. A joiner with a lower-level skill gives up its slot to the next level 5 joiner in line (kingshotguides.com). This page assumes all four are level 5 (the hero is 4-star or better), so each brings the full % on its card.

Assumed: in PvP, your damage dealt and damage taken count the same.
